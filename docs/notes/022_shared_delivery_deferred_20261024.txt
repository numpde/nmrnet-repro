../../../nmr-provider-tools/notes/014_joint_provider_refactor_decisions_20261028.txt
This note records NMRNet-specific follow-up; it does not replace those decisions.

1. Make ordinary updates practical without abandoning a selected result.
   The current up command requires an empty project; down waits for a clean
   provider exit before removing dependencies. Develop a shared lifecycle
   approach that permits safe routine updates and handles partial deployments
   without relying on forced removal.

2. Resolve blocked shutdowns without losing reporting duties. A selected result
   can keep shutdown waiting indefinitely while delivery is unavailable. Keep
   the original report and its owner until durable handoff, and develop an
   operator-visible recovery path with the shared delivery owners.

3. Qualify scientific and resource behavior separately from lifecycle tests.
   GPU, out-of-memory and numerical campaigns have not been established by the
   current lifecycle checks. Confirm the intended deployment and scientific
   claims before presenting those checks as evidence.

4. Preserve the distinction between public result capacity and protected
   evidence capacity. The result planner reports a conservative public size
   limit before prediction; oversized protected preparation evidence remains a
   separate retained failure and needs its own reviewed reporting policy.

5. Native recovery after supervisor replacement requires continuity of the
   original scratch authority or independent proof of its cleanup. An empty new
   scratch directory alone does not establish that the old child was cleaned up.

6. Source recovery has a bounded per-Attempt budget. NMRNet's implementation
   permits another Job after a classified Upload outage only when the original
   controller and factory have settled; the failed Job remains claimed. A
   two-Job test against intermediate provider-core source a4cb69fb exercised
   three signed Upload-list 503 responses, the retained first claim, a later
   completed Job, protected outage evidence and shutdown without a fabricated
   FAIL. The earlier 5aab1abd candidate also passed focused Upload-preparation
   and service checks; focused checks also passed against the final release wheel.
   Live deployment qualification remains outstanding. The shared design permits
   continuation only when the other Job has independent resources and capacity
   is available.
   A retained claim still consumes that finite capacity, so continuation alone
   cannot guarantee recovery from repeated outages: admission can eventually
   wait for capacity until this supervisor lifetime ends. A future policy for
   reconciling an Attempt known to have closed needs shared ownership review;
   elapsed time or absence from discovery does not release its claim. Do not
   infer or manufacture a terminal Attempt outcome or discard unresolved ownership.

7. Provider-core 0.5.0 does not classify a TLS handshake EOF as a temporary
   network failure. A settled Job read can consequently stop a provider, and
   terminal delivery can pause its binding rather than retrying the retained
   request. The shared owner is reviewing operation-specific recovery; retain
   TLS verification, the original failure and the API's exact replay rules.

8. NMRNet operator notices are best effort. An atomic pipe rejects a notice
   larger than its available record limit, and a full or broken sink can reject
   even a short notice. The process retains the latest failed notice and output
   error in memory and attempts to report them at shutdown; later failures can
   replace that record, and the shutdown notice can also fail. The terminal
   failure and worker cleanup do not depend on logging. Review a bounded way to
   signal omitted or unavailable diagnostics without claiming durable storage
   or introducing a local payload dump.
